SELFISHNET v2 - HOW IT WORKS
A plain-language guide to what this app does and how it does it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. WHAT THIS APP DOES
--------------------------------------------------------------------
SelfishNet v2 is a LAN (local network) traffic controller for Windows.
From one PC on your network you can:

  - See the devices connected to your local network (IP, MAC, name).
  - Limit how fast each device can download and upload.
  - Block a device's traffic completely.
  - Watch live bandwidth charts for the whole network and per device.

It works at a low level: instead of configuring the router, the app
temporarily convinces the other devices to send their traffic through
your computer. Your computer then forwards that traffic at the speed
you chose. When you press Stop (or close the app), everything is
restored.

Typical uses: testing how an app behaves on a slow connection,
limiting a guest device, or learning how networks work. Only use it on
networks you own or are authorized to test.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE CORE IDEA: ARP AND THE "MAN IN THE MIDDLE"
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Inside a LAN, devices do not talk by IP address alone. They need the
hardware address (MAC) of the destination. The protocol that maps
"IP address -> MAC address" is called ARP.

When a computer wants to send something to the router, it asks the
LAN: "Who has 192.168.1.1? Tell me your MAC." The router answers, and
the computer remembers that answer in its ARP cache for a short time.

ARP was designed in a simpler era and has no authentication: any
device can answer, and most systems simply believe the latest answer.

SelfishNet uses that. For each device you choose, it sends two small,
fake ARP answers:

  1. To the device:  "The router's MAC is MY MAC."
  2. To the router:  "That device's MAC is MY MAC."

Now both sides send their traffic to your computer instead of directly
to each other. Your computer is in the middle. That is why this
technique is called ARP spoofing, and the position is called a
man-in-the-middle.

This app repeats those fake answers regularly (every 2 seconds on the
Modern engine, every 5 seconds on the Legacy engine) because devices
refresh their ARP caches. If the app stops, devices eventually go back
to the truth on their own, but the app also restores everything
immediately (see section 6).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
3. HOW TRAFFIC IS FORWARDED AND LIMITED
--------------------------------------------------------------------
While the app is in the middle, it receives the traffic it tricked the
devices into sending. For every packet it decides:

  Download (router -> device):
    The packet arrived from the router and is addressed to a device we
    control. If the device is blocked, or over its limit, the packet
    is dropped. Otherwise the app rewrites the packet's destination
    MAC to the real device and sends it on its way.

  Upload (device -> router):
    The packet came from a controlled device. Same rule: drop if
    blocked or over the limit; otherwise rewrite the destination MAC
    to the real router and forward it.

  Traffic for your own PC:
    Counted in the statistics, never forwarded.

Two engines do this differently:

  LEGACY ENGINE
    A faithful port of the original 2007 program. Its limit is a hard
    1-second window: at the start of each second the device gets its
    full allowance; when it is used up, further packets are dropped
    until the next second. Simple and predictable, but bursty.

  MODERN ENGINE (default)
    Same spoofing and forwarding, smoother shaping. It uses a "token
    bucket": the allowance refills continuously (like a bucket filling
    with drops), and a packet is sent only if there is enough in the
    bucket. This avoids the on/off bursts of the old method. It also
    understands VLAN-tagged traffic and reacts instantly if a device
    tries to re-learn the real router behind our back.

You can switch engines at any time from the toolbar. The app stops
shaping, repairs the network, starts the other engine and resumes.
Devices, limits and names are kept.

Limits are entered in KByte/s or KBit/s (toggle in the toolbar);
0 means unlimited. Internally everything is stored in bytes per second,
so changing units never changes your limits.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
4. WHAT THE APP READS (AND WHAT IT DOES NOT)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
The app reads packet headers to learn addresses and measure speeds.
It does not decrypt traffic, does not store the content of packets,
and does not log websites. What it keeps on disk is only:

  - Your settings (language, theme, units, last adapter, engine).
  - Device names and limits, remembered by MAC address, so they come
    back next time.

Both files live in %AppData%\SelfishNetV2\.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
5. DEVICE DISCOVERY
--------------------------------------------------------------------
When it opens, and then quietly every 60 seconds, the app sends ARP
requests for every address in your subnet ("who is there?") and
listens for the answers. It also learns passively from the ARP traffic
that is already happening, so most new devices appear without any
action. The Discover button forces a scan immediately. Names come from
reverse DNS and can be renamed by double-clicking (stored by MAC, so
a new IP does not lose the name).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
6. LEAVING THE NETWORK CLEAN (HEALING)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Your devices' ARP caches still point at your computer when you stop.
The app fixes that by sending genuine ARP messages with the real
addresses:

  - 35 pairs of unicast corrections (device <-> router) per device.
  - 3 broadcast announcements for the router and 3 for each device,
    so even machines that never answered are corrected.

It remembers every device it actually poisoned, independently of the
checkboxes, so it never "forgets" to heal a device: unchecking a
device mid-session heals it immediately, and stopping, switching
engines, changing adapters or closing the app all run the same repair.

If the app is force-killed (Task Manager, power loss), it cannot heal.
That is not permanent: ARP entries expire on their own within seconds
to a few minutes, or immediately when a device asks again. A normal
Stop or Close always heals first.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
7. WHAT IT NEEDS TO RUN
--------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Windows 10 or 11. Builds are provided for 64-bit and 32-bit
    Windows (use the x64 one on a modern PC).
  - Administrator rights: sending raw network packets requires it.
    The app asks for elevation when it starts.
  - Npcap (https://npcap.com) or legacy WinPcap. Npcap is the modern,
    signed packet driver; the app detects it automatically. This is
    the only external component - the app itself is one portable exe
    with no .NET install needed.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
8. LIMITS AND KNOWN EDGE CASES
--------------------------------------------------------------------
  - One network adapter and one subnet at a time.
  - IPv4 only.
  - Devices must be on the same local network (same L2 segment).
  - Upload limiting depends on the device accepting our fake ARP
    answer. If a device ignores it, its uploads may bypass the limit
    while downloads still work.
  - Some Wi-Fi networks use "client isolation" or ARP protection on
    the router; there, spoofing cannot work at all (the network blocks
    device-to-device traffic). That is a router setting, not an app
    bug.
  - If Windows' own packet forwarding (IPEnableRouter) is enabled, the
    system may route traffic around the app, so limits may not apply.
    The app warns you; disable it for full control.
  - Mesh systems with several gateway MAC addresses are only partially
    supported (one gateway identity is tracked).
  - Your own PC's traffic is not limited by the app.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
9. QUICK START
--------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. Run SelfishNetV2.exe and accept the Administrator prompt.
  2. Choose your network adapter (the one with your LAN gateway).
  3. Devices appear automatically; use Discover if needed.
  4. Type a download/upload limit in the grid, or tick Block.
     Tick/un-tick Spoof to include/exclude a device (all included by
     default).
  5. Press Start Shaping. The status dot turns green.
  6. Press Stop before closing if you want the fastest repair; closing
     the app heals too.

Tips:
  - Keep the app open while shaping; closing it stops shaping.
  - If a limit seems ignored, press Start again after changing it (the
    engine reads limits live, but a fresh poison round helps stubborn
    devices).
  - The Legacy/Modern switch is the fastest way to compare behavior.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
10. SAFETY AND LEGAL NOTE
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Redirecting another device's traffic is intrusive. Use this only on
networks you own or have explicit permission to test. The authors take
no responsibility for misuse. The app always tries to restore the
network to its original state when stopped.
